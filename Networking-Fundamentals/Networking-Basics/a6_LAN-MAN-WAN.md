
### Classification of Networks Based on Geographical Area

A network can be classified on the basis of different factors, such as:

I. **Internet Connectivity:** Whether it uses internet for communication or not.

II. **Geographical Area:** Based on the area covered by the network.

III. **Transmission Medium:** Based on whether the network is wired or wireless.

Based on geographical area, networks are mainly classified into three types:

I. **LAN – Local Area Network**

II. **MAN – Metropolitan Area Network**

III. **WAN – Wide Area Network**

### 1. LAN (Local Area Network)

**Definition:** A Local Area Network (LAN) is a network that connects devices without internet within a limited geographical area, such as a room, home, office or building.

**Characteristics:**

I. **Coverage Area:** Covers a small geographical area, usually within the same building or premises.

II. **Communication:** Devices communicate with each other through wired or wireless connections without necessarily using the Internet.

III. **Devices Used:** Switches are commonly used to connect multiple devices within a LAN.

IV. **Speed:** LAN generally provides the highest speed among LAN, MAN and WAN.

V. **Security:** LAN can provide better control over network security because it is managed within a limited area. However, security depends on network configuration and protection mechanisms.

VI. **Transmission Media:** Commonly uses Ethernet cables (Twisted Pair), Fibre Optic Cables and Wi-Fi.

**Examples:**

- Connecting a laptop and mobile phone directly through a local network to transfer files.
- Connecting multiple computers within an office using switches.
- Connecting devices within a home through a Wi-Fi router.

### 2. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

**Definition:** A Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) connects multiple LANs across a larger geographical area, such as a city or metropolitan region.

**Characteristics:**

I. **Coverage Area:** Covers a larger geographical area than LAN, usually across a city or metropolitan region.

II. **Communication:** Connects multiple LANs located in different buildings or locations. Communication does not necessarily require the public Internet.

III. **Devices Used:** Routers and switches are commonly used to connect different network segments.

IV. **Speed:** Generally offers moderate speed compared to LAN and WAN, although actual speeds depend on the technology used.

V. **Security:** Security depends on the network infrastructure, access controls and encryption mechanisms.

VI. **Transmission Media:** Commonly uses Optical Fibre Cables and other high-speed communication links.

**Example:**

Suppose a college has three different buildings:

- Building A
- Building B
- Building C

If all three buildings are located at different places within a city and their LANs are interconnected through dedicated communication links without using the public Internet, it can be an example of MAN.

### 3. WAN (Wide Area Network)

**Definition:** A Wide Area Network (WAN) connects multiple networks through internet over a large geographical area, such as different cities, countries or continents.

**Characteristics:**

I. **Coverage Area:** Covers a large geographical area and can connect networks across different cities, countries and continents.

II. **Communication:** Connects multiple geographically separated networks using communication links and network infrastructure.

III. **Devices Used:** Routers are commonly used to connect different networks over WAN.

IV. **Speed:** WAN generally has higher latency and may offer lower speeds compared to LAN. However, actual bandwidth depends on the technology and infrastructure used.

V. **Security:** WAN requires strong security mechanisms because data may travel through shared or third-party communication infrastructure.

VI. **Transmission Media:** Commonly uses Optical Fibre Cables, leased lines, undersea cables and satellite communication.

**Examples:**

- The Internet is the largest example of a WAN.
- Two computers communicating through Facebook or WhatsApp using the Internet.
- Satellite-based Internet services such as Starlink.

### Comparison Between LAN, MAN and WAN

| Basis | LAN | MAN | WAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full Form | Local Area Network | Metropolitan Area Network | Wide Area Network |
| Coverage | Small area | City / Metropolitan area | Countries / Continents |
| Speed | Generally highest | Depends on infrastructure | Generally higher latency |
| Devices | Mainly Switches | Switches and Routers | Mainly Routers |
| Security | More control | Depends on infrastructure | Requires strong security |
| Transmission Media | Ethernet, Wi-Fi | Optical Fibre | Optical Fibre, Satellite, Leased Lines |
| Example | Home / Office Network | City-wide network | Internet |
